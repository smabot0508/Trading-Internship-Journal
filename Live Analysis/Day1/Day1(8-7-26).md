# US Market Analysis — July 8, 2026 (Wednesday)

**Work Format (effective 8th July onward):** Daily US market coverage during Indian evening–to–midnight hours, split into three tracked sessions.

| Session | IST Timing | Corresponding US Time (ET) | What I Do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-Market** | 5:00 PM – 7:00 PM | 7:30 AM – 9:30 AM | Read overnight/morning news, identify catalysts, form a directional prediction + key stocks to watch, and document it *before* the US market opens. |
| **Market Hours** | 7:00 PM – 1:30 AM | 9:30 AM – 4:00 PM | Track the live session and observe whether price action confirms or contradicts my Pre-Market prediction. Note the actual big movers and *why* they moved. |
| **After Hours** | 1:30 AM – 2:30 AM | 4:00 PM – 5:00 PM | Reconcile: did the Pre-Market call and the Market Hours observation actually match? Note fresh after-hours headlines that could carry into the next session, and log the final takeaway/strategy lesson. |

The point of this structure is to train prediction accuracy: I commit to a view *before* the open (Pre-Market), test it live (Market Hours), and grade myself *after* the close (After Hours) — rather than only describing what happened with hindsight.

## 1. Pre-Market Analysis (7:30 AM – 9:30 AM ET)

### Overnight/Morning Catalyst
The dominant story overnight was a sharp re-escalation between the US and Iran. Iran struck commercial vessels passing through the Strait of Hormuz, and the US responded with a fresh wave of strikes on Iranian targets. Speaking from Ankara ahead of a NATO summit, President Trump said the US–Iran ceasefire understanding was effectively over, while leaving the door open to further talks. The US Treasury also revoked its waiver permitting Iranian oil sales.

This immediately hit two markets:
- **Oil** – WTI and Brent crude both jumped over 3% in early trading on renewed Strait-of-Hormuz supply risk.
- **Risk assets** – S&P 500 futures were down close to 1%, Dow futures down roughly 1%, and Nasdaq futures also lower, with VIX futures spiking nearly 12% intraday (briefly above 18) before easing slightly.

### Supporting Factors
- The Fed's June meeting minutes (released the prior session) leaned hawkish — some officials flagged a case for a further rate hike given still-elevated inflation, which added to the risk-off tone.
- Chip stocks were already under pressure heading in: the PHLX Semiconductor Index (SOX) was down roughly 16% from its recent high, with names like Micron and Sandisk falling further pre-market.
- A handful of names were bucking the trend pre-market: Dollar Tree (upgraded by Goldman Sachs to neutral from sell) and energy/defense-adjacent names, which benefit directly from higher oil prices.
- SpaceX's addition to the Nasdaq-100 was a notable structural event, though the stock itself was sliding pre-market.

### My Prediction (made before the open)
- **Direction:** Broad risk-off open — I expect the Dow to lead losses (heavier industrial/blue-chip, rate-sensitive composition), with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq also opening lower.
- **Sector call:** Energy should be green on the oil spike (watch XOM, CVX). Semiconductors/tech should stay weak into the open, extending Tuesday's correction, given the hawkish Fed minutes plus the fresh geopolitical shock.
- **Watchlist:** XOM, CVX (oil-driven upside), MU, SNDK, INTC, AMD (further chip downside), DLTR (isolated upgrade-driven strength).

## 2. Market Hours Observation (9:30 AM – 4:00 PM ET)

### How the Open Played Out
The open matched the Pre-Market call almost exactly: Dow fell about 488 points (-0.9%) at the bell, S&P 500 down ~0.6%, Nasdaq down ~0.4%. Chip stocks initially extended their pre-market losses before staging a partial bounce roughly an hour into the session.

### Midday Escalation
Stocks actually pushed to fresh session lows around midday after further Trump comments raised the odds of continued strikes ("we'll probably hit them hard again tonight"). At the lows: S&P 500 -0.91% (7,435.81), Nasdaq -0.92% (25,584.75), Dow -1.51% (52,126.81). This was a deeper move than my Pre-Market prediction anticipated — the drawdown extended because the geopolitical situation kept deteriorating live during the session, not just at the open.

### Where My Prediction Broke Down: The Chip/Tech Reversal
This is the most important observation of the day. Despite my prediction that semiconductors/tech would stay weak, the sector staged a sharp intraday reversal:
- **Broadcom** rallied ~5-6% after expanding its US-made components agreement with Apple, and **Apple** itself rose almost 1% on the news.
- **Arista Networks** (+7%), **Akamai** (+7.9%), and **Alibaba** (+11.6%) also surged — largely on stock-specific catalysts (earnings, partnerships) rather than the macro theme.
- **Penguin Solutions** jumped +26.6% on an earnings beat.

This was strong enough to pull the **Nasdaq Composite into positive territory by the close (+0.2%)** — a full reversal from a red open, even though the macro backdrop (oil spike, hawkish Fed, Iran conflict) never actually improved during the day.

### What Stayed Weak (in line with the Pre-Market call)
- **Dow** closed down 576.76 points (-1.09%) to 52,348.39 — the worst-performing major index, as predicted.
- **S&P 500** closed -0.28% to 7,482.71.
- Credit-sensitive names fell: JPMorgan -2.5%, Visa -1.3%.
- AI hyperscalers were broadly weak on data-center spending concerns: Alphabet -1.4%, Amazon -1%, Microsoft -1.4%.
- Laggards elsewhere: Micron -4.7%, AMD -6.5%, Navitas Semiconductor -9%+ (hit by a competitor's patent-infringement suit), American Express -3.8%, Sherwin-Williams -3.5%, Boeing -3%.
- Energy call was correct: Exxon Mobil rallied on the oil spike, and the Energy sector was one of the only groups to finish green alongside Consumer Staples-adjacent defensive pockets in early trade (though Staples itself actually closed lower, -1.1%, by the end of the session — a reminder that early-session sector leadership isn't guaranteed to hold to the close).

### Cross-Market Context
- Oil settled the day up sharply: WTI +4.37% to $73.52, Brent +5.43% to $78.19.
- Gold extended its recent slide for a fourth straight session, staying under pressure even with the risk-off tone (gold has fallen more than 20% since the Iran conflict first began in February).
- 10-Year Treasury yield rose to 4.59%, its highest level since late May.
- VIX finished the day up 3.6% to 16.13 — a smaller gain than the pre-market spike (which briefly touched above 18), meaning realized volatility eased somewhat from the morning panic as the session progressed.

### After-Hours / Overnight Developments
Heading into the next session, overnight trading showed the semiconductor strength was not a one-day blip:
- Nasdaq 100 futures were already indicated roughly +1% overnight, with a broader semiconductor gauge up around 4%.
- **Micron Technology** announced plans to raise its US plant investment to $250 billion to meet AI-driven demand — a major forward-looking capex catalyst for the whole chip space.
- **SK Hynix's** US listing was reported to be more than 7x oversubscribed, signaling strong investor appetite for memory/AI-adjacent names specifically.
- Asian markets and European chip names (ASML, BE Semiconductor, STMicroelectronics) also firmed up in sympathy overnight.

This after-hours flow effectively confirmed that the Nasdaq's late intraday reversal was the start of a genuine short-term rotation into semiconductors — not just a single-stock news bounce — which matters directly for how I should read the next Pre-Market session.
