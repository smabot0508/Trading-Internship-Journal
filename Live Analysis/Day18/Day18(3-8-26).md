### Overnight/Morning Catalysts

- **BMY (Bristol-Myers Squibb)** — The Financial Times reported August 2 that BMY and AstraZeneca were in early-stage merger discussions. By this morning, follow-up reporting suggested the tie-up was "unlikely to happen," and AstraZeneca shares were down sharply (~8%) while BMY itself was only modestly lower — a classic case of a merger-speculation premium starting to unwind. This sits on top of an otherwise strong quarter: BMY beat Q2 estimates (adjusted EPS $2.04) and raised full-year guidance just days earlier, with BofA raising its price target to $71.
- **FERG (Ferguson Enterprises)** — Jumped roughly 7–9% pre-market and through the morning after being selected for **inclusion in the S&P 500**, replacing Electronic Arts (which is being acquired). This is a pure index-fund-flow catalyst — an estimated $20 trillion in capital is benchmarked to the S&P 500, and inclusion typically front-loads a sharp pop as the news hits, followed by continued but more gradual buying as funds actually rebalance.
- **LNTH (Lantheus Holdings)** — The single biggest catalyst of the day: Curium US LLC announced a **definitive agreement to acquire Lantheus for $102.50 per share in cash**, plus Contingent Value Rights worth up to $12.00/share more (total consideration up to $114.50), with the deal expected to close in the first half of **2027**. TD Cowen downgraded the stock to Hold same-day, arguing the current price already reflects the deal's value.
- **ATKR (Atkore)** — No news yet at the time of the trade — the stock was simply trading in an unusually tight range ahead of its own Q3 FY2026 earnings report, scheduled for before the bell the **very next morning** (August 4).

## 4. Trade Strategy — Actual Trades, Full CANSLIM Reasoning, and Results

> All four trades today were **short** — the first all-short session in this journal. None of these names were selected by growth/accumulation criteria, so each one is evaluated here on how it *actually* holds up against the CANSLIM framework, rather than having been pre-filtered by it.

### 🔴 BMY — SHORT — ✅ **PASSED**

**Why I took this trade:** This wasn't a CANSLIM-driven trade — BMY didn't clear any growth screener today. Read against the framework anyway: **C/A** are actually solid (a real Q2 beat, raised guidance), which is exactly why this trade wasn't a fundamental bet against the company. **N (New):** the "new" driver of today's price action was the AstraZeneca merger-speculation *cooling off*, not a fundamental deterioration — a fading rumor, not a fading business. **M:** the entire thesis was that a speculative merger premium tends to deflate once the market concludes the deal is unlikely, independent of the underlying company's health.

**How I planned the entry and exit:**
- **Entry (65.38):** short on the failure to hold the morning's bounce attempt, as the merger-speculation premium continued deflating.
- **Stop Loss (66.04):** above that failed bounce level — a 1.01% stop.
- **Target (64.60):** the next support level below.
- **Risk/Reward:** Risk $0.66 (1.01%) to make $0.78 (1.19%) → **RR of 1.18** — the tightest, most balanced RR of the day, appropriate for a speculation-driven (not fundamentally-driven) short.

**Result:** Price worked down through the target zone before stabilizing and grinding back higher into the close at **65.46**. Target hit during the session.

### 🔴 LNTH — SHORT — ✅ **PASSED**

**Why I took this trade:** A textbook merger-arbitrage short, and worth explaining as its own setup type since it's the first of this kind in the journal. Once a firm, all-cash deal is signed (here, $102.50/share from Curium, plus CVRs), the stock typically settles to trade at a small discount to the deal price — reflecting the time value of the roughly 10-month wait until closing (expected 1H 2027) and residual deal-completion risk, rather than trading right up against the headline number on announcement day. **The stop was placed essentially at the deal price itself (102.55, just above the $102.50 offer)** — a genuinely meaningful technical level in an M&A situation, since trading meaningfully *above* the offer would suggest the market expects a higher competing bid. TD Cowen's same-day downgrade to Hold, explicitly citing that the price already reflected the deal's value, directly supported fading any move too close to the cash number.

**How I planned the entry and exit:**
- **Entry (102.13):** short just below the deal price, on the read that the stock was trading too close to the $102.50 offer for a deal that won't close for roughly 10 months.
- **Stop Loss (102.55):** just above the $102.50 cash offer price itself.
- **Target (101.23):** a level reflecting a more typical time-value discount to the deal price.
- **Risk/Reward:** Risk $0.42 (0.41%) to make $0.90 (0.88%) → **RR of 2.14**.

**Result:** Price drifted steadily down through the target and continued lower, closing the session at **101.19** — right in line with the deal-discount thesis. Target hit and slightly exceeded.

### 🔴 ATKR — SHORT — ❌ **STOPPED OUT**

**Why I took this trade:** This is the session's clearest cautionary tale, and it's worth being fully honest about it. ATKR hadn't reported earnings yet — the report was due before the bell the very next morning — and the trade was a bet that the stock would keep drifting inside its unusually tight pre-earnings range rather than break out. **This is the same mistake flagged on Day 17 with TEAM**: shorting a quiet consolidation immediately ahead of a known catalyst, on the assumption that "quiet" means "no move coming," when a tight range ahead of an earnings date is just as easily the market coiling before a large move in *either* direction.

**How I planned the entry and exit:**
- **Entry (93.54):** short inside the tight pre-earnings range, betting on continuation of the narrow chop.
- **Stop Loss (93.61):** just above the range high — a very tight 0.08% stop, appropriately small given how little conviction this trade actually had.
- **Target (93.36):** the range low.
- **Risk/Reward:** Risk $0.07 (0.08%) to make $0.18 (0.19%) → **RR of 2.57** on paper, though the tiny absolute size of both numbers reflects how little real edge this trade had.

**Result:** A single powerful reversal candle broke sharply above the range late in the session, taking out the stop at **93.61**. Stopped out. **In hindsight, this is a striking result:** Atkore's actual report the next morning brought both an earnings beat *and* a surprise all-cash acquisition by Prysmian at $95.00/share, sending shares up over 26% in pre-market trading. Whether this session's late reversal candle reflected the market genuinely sensing something ahead of the news or was simple coincidence, the lesson stands either way — shorting a quiet range directly ahead of a scheduled catalyst is exactly the kind of setup where a small, sharp move against the position should be expected as a real possibility, not treated as noise.

## Key Takeaways From Day 18

1. **This was the first day built entirely around real corporate events rather than a growth screener** — a merger-speculation unwind (BMY), an index-inclusion pop (FERG), a signed cash acquisition (LNTH), and a stock sitting quietly ahead of its own earnings (ATKR). CANSLIM still applies as a lens for reading *why* the underlying business is or isn't strong, but today's actual trade triggers came entirely from event flow, not from C-A-N-S-L-I-M screening — worth keeping both toolkits active rather than treating them as competing approaches.
2. **LNTH is a new, well-defined setup type worth formalizing: the merger-arbitrage short.** Once a firm cash deal is signed, the offer price becomes a hard technical ceiling, and the trade becomes about the appropriate time-value discount rather than about growth, earnings, or any other CANSLIM letter. This is a genuinely different kind of edge than anything else traded in this journal so far.
3. **ATKR is the same lesson as Day 17's TEAM, wearing a different costume: don't short a quiet, tight range sitting directly in front of a scheduled catalyst.** The position was sized appropriately small for the low conviction involved, which kept the damage to a fraction of a percent — but the more important fix is recognizing the setup type itself (tight range + imminent catalyst = coiled spring, not "safe to fade") before the trade is taken, not just after a small loss confirms it.
4. **Three for four with every trade on the short side is a good reminder that direction isn't the variable that matters — setup quality is.** BMY, FERG, and LNTH all had clear, falsifiable reasons for the fade (a cooling rumor, an overextended pop, a capped deal price); ATKR had none beyond "it's been quiet," and that's exactly the trade that didn't work.
5. **Next Pre-Market session:** revisit whether the CANSLIM Screener or the Pre-Market Move screener produces the better trade set on a day-by-day basis — today suggests event-driven gap screening can surface genuinely strong, well-reasoned trades when each name's *specific* catalyst is understood, not just its price movement.
