## 1. Pre-Market Analysis (7:30 AM – 9:30 AM ET)

### Overnight Catalyst
Coming into Thursday, the semiconductor space was the clear story. Overnight, **Micron Technology** announced it is accelerating its US fab and technology investment plan to **more than $250 billion through 2035**, tied to surging demand for memory chips used in AI infrastructure. Alongside this, **SK Hynix's** US listing was reported to be more than **7x oversubscribed**, signaling very strong investor appetite for memory/AI-adjacent names specifically.

This followed two rough sessions of profit-taking in chip stocks (the sector was down as much as 16% from its recent high going into Wednesday), so the setup going into Thursday was: **a beaten-down sector + a large, sector-wide bullish catalyst.**

### Pre-Market Readings
- Nasdaq 100 futures were indicated **+1%** overnight; a broader semiconductor futures gauge was up around **+4%**.
- **Micron (MU)** rose **+3.5% pre-market to $982.05**, with Bank of America analyst Vivek Arya reiterating a bullish rating.
- **SanDisk (SNDK)** and other memory names were higher pre-market too, aided by Goldman Sachs turning more bullish on AI memory stocks ahead of earnings.
- Chip stocks broadly — **Nvidia, AMD, Intel, Micron, Western Digital, SanDisk** — were all indicated higher pre-market as investors rotated back into the "AI hardware trade."
- One notable divergence flagged pre-market: reports that a Chinese AI firm (DeepSeek) was building its own custom AI chip to reduce reliance on Nvidia — a stock-specific overhang unique to **NVDA** even as the rest of the sector looked strong.
- European chip names (ASML, BE Semiconductor, STMicroelectronics) and Asian markets (SK Hynix, Nikkei, Kospi) also firmed up in sympathy overnight, reinforcing this wasn't a single-stock move.

### How I Picked My Watchlist From Pre-Market
I used the following filter logic before the open:
1. **Start from the catalyst, not the ticker.** The Micron capex headline was sector-wide, so I first listed every liquid name that catalyst would plausibly move: MU, SNDK, NVDA, plus the two index proxies I use to confirm whether a move is a genuine sector rotation or a single-stock story — **QQQ** (Nasdaq-100 exposure) and **SPY** (broad market confirmation).
2. **Check pre-market % move and relative volume** on each — MU and SNDK were both showing unusually strong pre-market gains versus their normal pre-market range, which told me real money was already positioning before the bell.
3. **Look for a name that does *not* fit the story.** NVDA was flagged specifically because it was lagging the sector pre-market on the DeepSeek/custom-chip headline — I kept it on the watchlist deliberately as a potential "fade the laggard" or "wait and watch" case rather than a long, since a name moving against its own sector's catalyst is exactly the kind of divergence worth tracking.
4. **Add the index ETFs as confirmation tools**, not primary trades — if QQQ and SPY were also green pre-market, that told me the semiconductor strength had a real chance of lifting the broader tape, not just staying isolated to two or three tickers.

This gave me a 5-stock watchlist: **MU, SNDK, NVDA, QQQ, SPY.**

## 2. Market Hours Observation (9:30 AM – 4:00 PM ET)

The session played out largely in line with the pre-market setup: chip strength led the tape, oil eased back (removing some of the prior day's risk-off pressure), and the broader market followed the semiconductor leadership higher.
- **Nasdaq Composite** ended the day **+0.8%**.
- **S&P 500** ended **+0.6%**.
- **Dow Jones Industrial Average** added about **138 points (+0.3%)** — a much smaller gain than the other two indices, since it carries far less semiconductor weight.
- The **VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH)** climbed roughly **+3%**, led by **Micron's** intraday gain (as high as **+6.9%** versus the Information Technology sector at one point).
- Investors rotated **into** semiconductors and **away from** the "Magnificent Seven" hyperscaler names — the Roundhill Magnificent Seven ETF (MAGS) was actually down about -0.6% on the day, confirming this was a sector-specific rotation, not a blanket "everything tech" rally.

### Stock-by-Stock Market Hours Momentum

**Micron (MU)** — Gapped up on the capex headline and BofA reiteration, then continued to grind higher through the session as the SMH-wide rally gave it sector tailwind on top of its own stock-specific catalyst. This is a textbook "stock-specific news + sector rotation" combination — the strongest type of long setup because two independent forces are pushing the same direction.

**SanDisk (SNDK)** — Similar profile to MU: benefited from the Goldman Sachs upgrade cycle on AI memory names ahead of earnings, plus the same sector-wide capex optimism. Momentum was strong and persistent through the day rather than a single spike-and-fade.

**Nvidia (NVDA)** — This is the important divergence of the day. Despite being in the same sector as MU/SNDK and despite bullish analyst commentary elsewhere (Goldman calling NVDA a "bargain," TD Cowen naming it a top pick), the stock actually **underperformed its own sector** and drifted lower through the session — consistent with the pre-market DeepSeek/custom-chip overhang weighing specifically on Nvidia's China-demand narrative, even while memory names (which aren't exposed to that specific headline) ripped higher.

**QQQ** — Tracked the Nasdaq's broad tech strength, up for the day, confirming the semiconductor rotation was strong enough to lift the index-level tape and not just isolated to two or three names.

**SPY** — Also green, but by a smaller margin than QQQ — consistent with the Dow's much smaller gain, reflecting that the rally was concentrated in tech/semis rather than broad-based across all sectors.

## 4. Trade Strategy Per Stock (Step-by-Step)

### 🟢 MU (Micron) — LONG
1. **Why picked:** Overnight $250B capex announcement (sector-wide bullish catalyst) + BofA reiteration on the same stock + sector already technically oversold (down 16% from highs) — three reasons stacking in the same direction.
2. **Entry:** On a pullback to the pre-market gap-fill zone (~$965–970) *or* on a breakout above the pre-market high ($982) with rising volume — I favor the breakout entry on a day with this strong a catalyst, since pullback entries risk missing the move on strong-momentum gap days.
3. **Stop Loss:** Below Wednesday's close ($948.80) — a clean, obvious level; if MU trades back below its own pre-catalyst closing price, the "this catalyst is driving the stock" thesis is broken.
4. **Target 1:** $1,000 (round-number psychological resistance).
5. **Target 2 / Trail:** If Target 1 breaks with volume, trail the stop using the rising 9-EMA on the 15-minute chart rather than taking a hard second target, since momentum names on strong catalyst days often keep extending.
6. **Exit plan:** Scale out roughly half the position at Target 1, trail the rest.

### 🟢 SNDK (SanDisk) — LONG
1. **Why picked:** Goldman Sachs turned more bullish on AI memory names ahead of earnings, and SNDK was showing the largest pre-market relative-volume spike of the group — a sign of real accumulation, not just sympathy buying.
2. **Entry:** Breakout above the pre-market high, confirmed by the first 15-minute candle closing above that level (avoids getting caught in an opening-range fakeout).
3. **Stop Loss:** Below the low of the first 15-minute candle (protects against a failed breakout without giving back too much of the move).
4. **Target:** Prior swing-high resistance level on the daily chart (the most recent high before the multi-day pullback) — using a prior structural level rather than a round number here, since SNDK doesn't have the same "psychological round number" behavior MU does at these price levels.
5. **Exit plan:** Full exit at target unless volume is clearly still expanding, in which case trail with a tighter stop instead of taking full profit.

### 🔴 NVDA (Nvidia) — SHORT / FADE THE LAGGARD
1. **Why picked:** Pre-market divergence — lagging its own sector on a stock-specific overhang (reports of a Chinese AI firm developing a custom chip to reduce Nvidia dependence), even while bullish analyst notes existed elsewhere. This is a "don't fight the divergence" setup, not a fundamental bearish call.
2. **Entry:** On a failed bounce/rejection at the security's VWAP or 9-EMA on the 15-minute chart during market hours — waiting for a rejection rather than shorting immediately protects against shorting straight into the broader sector's strength.
3. **Stop Loss:** Above the day's high at the time of entry (a rejection-based short is invalidated the moment price reclaims that level).
4. **Target:** Prior intraday support / the low of the pre-market range — this is a relative-weakness trade, not a "big breakdown" trade, so the target is modest and the position should be sized accordingly.
5. **Exit plan:** Take profit fully at target; this is a same-day, catalyst-specific trade, not a multi-day short thesis.

### ⚪ QQQ — CONFIRMATION / HEDGE INSTRUMENT (not a primary directional trade)
1. **Why picked:** Used to confirm whether the MU/SNDK strength was a genuine sector rotation capable of lifting the broader Nasdaq, or an isolated 2-stock story.
2. **How I use it:** If QQQ is green and rising in the first 30 minutes alongside MU/SNDK, that's confirmation to size up the MU/SNDK longs. If QQQ is flat or red while MU/SNDK are green, that's a signal the move may be too narrow to trust with full size.
3. **No independent entry/stop/target here** — it's a filter, not a trade, in this particular setup.

### ⚪ SPY — BROAD MARKET CONTEXT
1. **Why picked:** Same logic as QQQ, but for the broad market rather than tech specifically. A weaker SPY move relative to QQQ (which is exactly what happened — +0.83% vs +1.66%) tells me the rally is concentrated in tech/semis, not broad-based — useful context for risk management on the individual stock trades (a narrow rally is more fragile than a broad one).
4. **No independent entry/stop/target here** — used for market-breadth context only.

## Key Takeaways From Day 2

1. A strong, sector-wide catalyst (Micron's $250B capex plan) combined with a beaten-down starting point (semis down 16% from highs) produced one of the cleanest long setups of the week — MU and SNDK both held their gains through market hours and into after-hours.
2. Not every stock in a "hot" sector moves with the sector — NVDA's stock-specific overhang (China/DeepSeek custom-chip headline) kept it red all day even as its own peers rallied hard. Divergence-spotting in Pre-Market paid off here.
3. Using QQQ and SPY as confirmation/filter instruments (rather than primary trades) is a useful way to judge whether a 2–3 stock move is broad enough to trust with full position size.
4. The After-Hours "Most Active by Share Volume" list (dominated by leveraged/inverse ETFs like SOXS) can be read as an indirect sentiment confirmation tool — SOXS being heavily traded and down further supports the "semis were strongly bid" thesis from a different angle.
5. Next Pre-Market session: watch whether MU/SNDK strength continues or starts to fade, and whether NVDA's relative weakness resolves (either catches up to the sector or breaks down further on continued China-related headlines).
