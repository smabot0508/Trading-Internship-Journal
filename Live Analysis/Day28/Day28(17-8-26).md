> **New concept today: the 9 EMA / VWAP Crossover.** Every trade this session was planned around the same trigger — the 9-period EMA crossing the session's VWAP (and price confirming that cross), rather than a chart pattern (triangle, wedge) or an SMC liquidity concept. It's a momentum-confirmation setup: when the fast-moving average crosses the volume-weighted "fair value" line, it signals that short-term buying or selling pressure has genuinely shifted, not just drifted.

## 4. Trade Strategy — Actual Trades, Full CANSLIM + Technical Reasoning, and Results

> **Concept used across all four trades: 9 EMA / VWAP Crossover.** Long entries were taken when the 9 EMA crossed above VWAP (or price reclaimed both after dipping below), signaling short-term buying pressure taking control; short entries were taken on the mirror-image bearish cross. Three trades passed cleanly; one (MRVL) reversed hard after the initial breakout and hit its stop.

### 🔴 MRVL — LONG — ❌ **STOPPED OUT**

**Why I took this trade:** The 9 EMA crossed above VWAP early in the session, confirming that short-term momentum had flipped positive after the prior lower-range chop. No fresh company-specific catalyst today; this was a pure continuation read on the AI-memory-platform strength that drove MRVL's 14% spike back on Day 25.

**How I planned the entry and exit:**
- **Entry (235.62):** on confirmation of the 9 EMA/VWAP crossover, buying the reclaim above both lines.
- **Stop Loss (234.33):** below the crossover zone — a 0.55% stop.
- **Target (238.21):** the next resistance extension above.
- **Risk/Reward:** Risk $1.29 (0.55%) to make $2.59 (1.10%) → **RR of 2.01**.

**Result:** Price pushed briefly higher after the crossover but failed to hold, reversing hard and breaking back below the entry zone, triggering the stop at **234.33**. **Worth being direct about this one:** the crossover confirmed real short-term momentum, but with no fresh catalyst behind it, that momentum had nothing to sustain it once the initial buying interest was absorbed — a good reminder that a technical trigger alone, without a supporting "why," is the weakest version of this setup.

## Key Takeaways From Day 28

1. **MRVL vs. MU is the sharpest lesson of the day: the exact same technical trigger (9 EMA/VWAP crossover) produced opposite outcomes depending on whether real fundamental fuel was behind it.** MU's crossover was backed by a major catalyst (crossing $1,000, aggressive analyst target hikes, Soros's top holding); MRVL's crossover had no fresh story behind it and reversed once the initial buying interest was absorbed. Worth treating the crossover as a timing tool that still needs a "why" behind it, not a standalone signal.
2. **PLTR's bearish crossover working cleanly continues the trend from Day 27's clean resistance-rejection short** — after Day 23's round-trip and Day 26's stop-out, this is now two consecutive successful short attempts on the same name, suggesting the more precise, momentum-confirmed triggers (crossover, clean rejection) are outperforming the earlier, looser liquidity-sweep timing on this specific stock.
3. **MU's trade is this journal's strongest example yet of a crossover, a chart pattern, and the fundamentals all pointing the same direction simultaneously** — current-quarter strength, forward guidance, fresh analyst conviction, and heavyweight institutional sponsorship, confirmed by the exact same technical trigger used across the other three trades.
4. **The single loss (MRVL) cost the least of the four trades, consistent with this journal's usual pattern** — a tight, catalyst-free crossover was sized appropriately small relative to MU's catalyst-backed version of the same setup.
5. **Next Pre-Market session:** watch whether MRVL's reversal continues into a real pullback or was just a shakeout, and keep tracking MU closely given how large the new price targets are relative to today's move — there's a lot of room left if the market keeps validating the story, and a lot of room to disappoint if it doesn't.
