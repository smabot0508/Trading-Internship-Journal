## 1. Pre-Market Analysis (7:30 AM – 9:30 AM ET)

### Overnight/Morning Catalyst
Friday's setup was quieter than Thursday's, but had two specific things to watch:
- **SK Hynix's US listing debut** — South Korea's memory-chip giant (Micron's chief HBM rival) made its Nasdaq debut Friday, upsized to raise as much as **$29.4 billion**. A large, competing memory-chip IPO landing the same week as Micron's capex headlines meant investor capital in the memory trade had a new place to go.
- **Broad futures were softer overnight** — S&P 500 futures were down about **0.22%**, Nasdaq futures down about **0.36%**, as investors stayed cautious on the still-unresolved Middle East backdrop and a hawkish-leaning inflation picture, even with oil cooling off from Wednesday/Thursday's spike.
- **Micron (MU) pulled back roughly 1.8% pre-market**, giving back part of Thursday's 4.5% rally — read as profit-taking/capital rotation rather than a reversal of the thesis, since MU remained in a clearly bullish structure (trading ~9.7% above its 50-day moving average).
- Earnings season backdrop: **Delta Air Lines** reported Q2 results Friday, an early read on how the market would react to the start of earnings season more broadly.

## 2. Market Hours Observation (9:30 AM – 4:00 PM ET)

### NVDA — Broke Out of Its Multi-Day Range
After lagging the sector on Day 2, NVDA finally moved — two clean impulsive legs up (from a sweep low near **199.5**, up through **204**, and on to **211**) separated by tight consolidations. This is a genuine change in character versus Wednesday/Thursday's sideways/weak price action, and it printed right into the **211.00** resistance level by the end of the session.

### SNDK — Liquidity Sweep Then Reclaim
Pushed up to retest the **~1,940–1,945** resistance zone (matching the prior swing high), got swept down hard to a low of **1,896.65** — just barely holding above the 1,895 support/stop level — then recovered back above **1,900**, closing the session around **1,917–1,921**. This is a clean sweep-and-reclaim: the zone did its job as support even after tagging the resistance above it first.

### MU — Continuation of the Bearish Structure
Consistent with the Day 2 failed-breakout thesis: price consolidated in the **990–995** area (tagging a high of 994.99, just under the 995 invalidation level) before dropping sharply to **978**, extending the post-Day-2 downtrend and moving closer to the **960 / 948.80** target zone.

### QQQ / SPY — Broad Confirmation Holding
Both continued in clean uptrend structure (higher-highs, higher-lows) through the session, with QQQ once again the stronger of the two — consistent with the "tech-led, not broad-based" character of the rally that's held since Day 2.

## Key Takeaways From Day 3

1. **SNDK** validated the "sweep-and-reclaim" pattern cleanly — tagging a target *before* a sharp pullback that stopped just short of the stop level is a good example of why a stop should be based on structure, not just "how far the price moved against me."
2. **MU's** continuation to the downside directly validates the Day 2 lesson (breakout without a prior liquidity sweep tends to fail) — the stock has now round-tripped from a failed breakout into a genuine downtrend, exactly the kind of follow-through that confirms the earlier read.
3. **NVDA** finally showing life after being the Day 2 laggard is worth tracking closely — but tagging a level intraday isn't the same as confirming it. This is a good real-time example of why the entry rule should require a **close** above the trigger, not just a wick touching it.
4. **Next Pre-Market session (Monday, July 13):** the first thing to check is whether NVDA's Friday high holds as support on the open (confirms the breakout) or gets rejected back into the range (breakout failed) — that single data point will set the tone for how to treat the rest of the watchlist.
