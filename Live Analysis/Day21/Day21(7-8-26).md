### The Setup Type — SMC Liquidity Sweeps, Both Directions

Every trade today used the same **Smart Money Concepts (SMC) liquidity-sweep** logic that has anchored the last several sessions: price is pushed to a level where a cluster of resting stop-losses or breakout orders sits (a prior swing high or low), that level gets swept to trigger the resting orders, and the reversal that follows is treated as the real signal — not the sweep itself. The difference today was directional mix: **SNOW and MDB were both sweep-of-the-low, reversal-to-the-upside longs**, while **FTK was a sweep-of-the-high, reversal-to-the-downside short** — the mirror image. MDB is the reminder built into this framework from the start: a liquidity sweep is a probability read, not a guarantee, and today it didn't hold.

### Overnight/Morning Catalysts

**Result:** Price rallied off the reversal candle and pushed through the target into the 320–321 zone before fading back into the close. Target hit.

## Key Takeaways From Day 21

1. **MDB is the headline story: the first loss in three sessions, and it came from the exact setup that had been working.** The liquidity sweep triggered correctly, but the reversal simply didn't hold — a clean reminder that "sweep, then reverse" is a probability edge, not a certainty, and that the 1.39 RR on this trade was the lowest of the day for a reason worth respecting next time a similar setup appears with a compressed reward relative to risk.
2. **Today tested the framework in both directions on the same day for the first time.** SNOW and MDB were both sweep-of-the-low longs; FTK was a sweep-of-the-high short. Running the setup both ways in one session is a better test of whether the edge is real than only ever trading it from one side.
3. **FTK's result shows the payoff structure of this strategy working as designed.** A single 5.58% winner on a well-defined sweep more than covered the 1.55% loss on MDB — the entire point of insisting on RR above 2 before taking a trade.
4. **MDB's wider-than-usual stop (1.55% vs. the more typical 0.3–0.9% range used on SNOW and FTK) is worth flagging as a process note.** Wider stops on more violent candles are sometimes unavoidable, but they also compress risk/reward — this trade's 1.39 RR was already a caution sign before the outcome confirmed it.
5. **Next Pre-Market session:** Watch whether MDB's failed bounce extends into a deeper pullback or whether the stock reclaims the flush zone, and whether SNOW's and FTK's completed setups leave fresh liquidity levels worth tracking for the next round of sweeps.
