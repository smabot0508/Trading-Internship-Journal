### 🔴 MRVL — SHORT — ⚪ **CLOSED MANUALLY (sideways, small gain)**

**Why I took this trade:** **C/A/N** are all genuinely strong here — a real new AI memory product platform (not just sentiment), a secondary FCC-related tailwind, and a Buy-rated stock with a healthy balance sheet (debt-to-equity ~0.27) underneath the AI-hype pricing. This was, once again, a "fade the extension, not the fundamentals" trade — after a 14% single-day catalyst spike in a name already trading at a rich price-to-free-cash-flow multiple, a short-term pullback was the higher-probability read, consistent with the FERG/BLZE/FTK pattern used throughout this journal.

**How I planned the entry and exit:**
- **Entry (221.56):** short on the rejection after the initial spike.
- **Stop Loss (223.45):** above that rejection high — a 0.85% stop.
- **Target (218.57):** the next support level below.
- **Risk/Reward:** Risk $1.89 (0.85%) to make $2.99 (1.35%) → **RR of 1.58** as planned.

**Result:** Rather than resolving cleanly, price chopped in an unusually wide, volatile range for most of the session — consistent with the stock's high-beta character on a major catalyst day — without decisively tagging either the stop or the target. **The position was closed manually around 221.10**, banking a small gain rather than holding through the chop. As it turned out, a sharp decline arrived in the session's final stretch that would have carried well past the original target — but only after the trade was already closed, echoing Day 24's DELL outcome almost exactly.

## Key Takeaways From Day 25

1. **HPE is a clean example of the flip-direction discipline this journal has used repeatedly (SNOW Days 15–16, PLTR Day 24)** — rather than treating Day 23's stopped-out short as a grudge against the stock, today's trade simply followed the continuation the market was actually showing.
2. **MRVL is now the second straight instance of a manually-closed trade being vindicated by a move that arrived just after the exit (DELL on Day 24, now MRVL here).** That's worth watching as a pattern rather than dismissing as bad luck twice — it may suggest range-chop trades on high-beta names deserve either a wider stop/target from the start, or a pre-set time-based exit rule rather than an open-ended manual judgment call.
3. **MRVL's setup is a strong real-world instance of "fade the extension, not the fundamentals"** — a genuine new product platform (not vague sentiment) driving a 14% spike, on a name whose underlying financials (healthy balance sheet, real free cash flow) support the long-term bull case even while the short-term price spike was the actual target.
4. **Three consecutive sessions now (Days 23–25) have avoided a full stop-loss loss**, with Day 23's HPE stop-out being the last clean loss recorded. Worth treating as encouraging without assuming it's the new normal — Day 21's MDB is a reminder that losses are a normal, expected part of the process.
5. **Next Pre-Market session:** watch whether MRVL's genuine AI-catalyst strength reasserts itself after today's chop, and continue tracking whether manually-closed "sideways" trades keep showing this same pattern of the real move arriving just after the exit.
