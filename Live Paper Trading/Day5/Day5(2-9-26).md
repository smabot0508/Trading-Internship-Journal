### ✅ Trade 3 — PLTR Short, 5 shares (+$0.75)

**What went wrong:** DELL was actually basing/bottoming here, not breaking down — this was the first sign the stock had strong buying underneath it. DELL finished the day **up 9.06%**, so this short was positioned against what turned into a major uptrend day.

### ❌ Trade 5 — DELL Short, 5 shares (-$7.20)
Re-shorted 5 shares at $461.27 shortly after Trade 4 was stopped out, expecting a second chance at the downside. Stopped out again at $462.71 for a larger $7.20 loss.

**What went wrong:** This was doubling down on the same losing thesis right after the market had already shown (via Trade 4's stop-out) that DELL had strength. Instead of stepping aside, the size and risk were kept the same and the stop distance was wider, so the second loss was bigger than the first. DELL continued climbing hard afterward, up into the $467 area.

## Key Takeaways From Day 5

1. **The two DELL shorts were the costliest mistake pattern** — fighting a stock that finished the day up over 9% is a strong reminder to respect strength instead of assuming a pullback will turn into a breakdown, and especially not to re-enter the same losing side a second time right after being stopped out.
2. **The PLTR long was a pure chase** — buying at the top of a spike with no pullback and price already extended above VWAP is exactly the kind of entry that needs to be avoided; it produced the single largest loss of the day.
3. **Position sizing amplified the damage** — the two biggest losses (DELL Trade 5 and PLTR Trade 6) were also the two largest position sizes (5 and 10 shares), while the smallest loss (PLTR Trade 2) was sized at just 1 share. Worth deliberately sizing down on lower-confidence entries.
4. **The two winning trades shared a pattern** — both were entries taken with the existing trend/momentum (ZETA long into strength, PLTR short after confirmed breakdown) and both took profit at a pre-set target instead of over-holding.
5. **Next session:** before re-entering a stock after a stop-out, treat that stop as information about the stock's actual strength/weakness rather than "bad luck" to immediately re-try; and add a rule against entering right at a fresh intraday high/low without at least one pullback for confirmation.
